THE RIVERS RUN






  

    
How to play

    
The goal. Break the rival's base at the top. Troops that reach an enemy edge strike the base and are spent, harder the costlier and healthier they arrive. Three Pentagons guard each base; while they stand, troops fight them first. When one falls, the others take on part of its strength, so the next is harder than the last (not in the first two fights). Every card you play is paid in your slaves' blood, and it soaks into your ground. Your Pentagons hold back its smell; once all three fall, the rival's parasites come for it.

    
Blood. Blood pays for every card and builds on its own, faster the more you have hurt the rival. Every kill refunds half the dead unit's cost to its killer, so trades move value instead of wasting it.

    
Cards. You carry 8 and hold 4. Playing one sends it to the back and the dashed card shows what comes next. Place by dragging a card onto your side, tapping a card then the field, or tapping a card and holding on the field to see its reach first. You deploy anywhere from the near river bank down; spells land anywhere.

    
Shapes tell you what a unit does.
      
✕
Melee troop: fights up close

      
➤
Ranged troop: shoots from a distance

      
☋
Leach: latches onto troops and drains them

      
◎
Armed building: holds its ground and fires

      
▦
Wall: soaks damage, does none

    

    
Rivers and bridges. Troops cannot wade: they cross only on the bridges. Once you have beaten Veyra you can seal one of your own bridges by tapping it (it costs nothing, but there is a cooldown). Anything standing on it drops into the river. Your bridge pulses when that would drown the rival's troops. No building may stand at either end of a bridge.

    
Spells. Spells land anywhere and affect everything whose body touches their circle. Some catch your own troops too. The Bestiary in your Collection explains each one.

    
Rivals. Each challenger openly breaks a rule you must keep, and the screen before each fight says which.

    
Trinkets. Some challengers leave a trinket behind. Carry it from the Trinket row on the screen before a fight.

    
Formations. With Grael's Crown, a relic, the deck picker lets you bind two troops into one card with a shape: one behind, beside or ahead of the other. It lands both at once and costs both, and it takes two slots. Its troops cannot be carried alone until you separate it with its cross.

    
Skirmish and Alchemy. Wanderers roam the rivers after Veyra. Fight them in Skirmish, from Battle: nothing in the story moves, and they carry materials. Materials and two spells you own can be brewed into something new.

    
The Holdfast. An optional mode, opened from Battle once Osric has fallen, best played with your phone on its side. Hold the Keep against raids that do not stop. Whatever you hold out for is paid into your pot; leave between waves to keep all of it. The story never needs it.

    
Your camp. Once you have a camp, open it from the menu and tap a building to work in it. Buildings are raised with Mortar. What each one does, and what they do for each other, is yours to find out.

Watch

    
Two armies meet while you watch from the hill, both played by the AI. Bring any cards here, even ones you have not won: your own saved decks, the presets, a friend's code, or a random draw.

    

    

    

    
    
  





  

    
Your decks

    
Build a deck of up to eight cards from anything in the game and save it under a name. Saved decks can be sent into Watch from either side.

    
    

    

    
    

    
  





  

    
Friends

    
Nobody here fights in person: you send armies out to fight on your behalf. Give a friend your army's code and they can send it into the field against theirs; paste a friend's code and their army marches against you. Nothing goes online, and the code carries only the names of your cards: no name, no message, nothing typed.

Alchemy

    
Two spells and a handful of materials make a new spell, cheaper than both and with a nature of its own. One brew makes three doses. The first time you cast the spell in a fight spends one, and after that it is free to cast for the rest of that fight. When they run out, brew again. Materials come from Skirmish wins and from a challenger's first fight.
