BLOOD IS DRAWN






  

    
How to play

    
The goal. Break the rival's base at the top. Troops that reach an enemy edge strike the base and are spent, harder the costlier and healthier they arrive. Three Pentagons guard each base; while they stand, troops fight them first. When one falls, the others take on part of its strength, so the next is harder than the last (not in the first two fights). Every card you play is paid in your slaves' blood, and it soaks into your ground. Your Pentagons hold back its smell; once all three fall, the rival's parasites come for it.

    
Blood. Blood pays for every card and builds on its own, faster the more you have hurt the rival. Every kill refunds half the dead unit's cost to its killer, so trades move value instead of wasting it.

    
Cards. You carry 8 and hold 4. Playing one sends it to the back and the dashed card shows what comes next. Place by dragging a card onto your side, tapping a card then the field, or tapping a card and holding on the field to see its reach first. You deploy anywhere from the near river bank down; spells land anywhere.

    
Shapes tell you what a unit does.
      
✕
Melee troop: fights up close

      
➤
Ranged troop: shoots from a distance

      
☋
Leach: latches onto troops and drains them

      
◎
Armed building: holds its ground and fires

      
▦
Wall: soaks damage, does none

    

    
Rivers and bridges. Troops cannot wade: they cross only on the bridges. Once you have beaten Veyra you can seal one of your own bridges for 2 blood by tapping it. Anything standing on it drops into the river. Your bridge pulses when that would drown the rival's troops. No building may stand at either end of a bridge.

    
Spells. Spells land anywhere and affect everything whose body touches their circle. Some catch your own troops too. The Card guide explains each one.

    
Rivals. Each challenger openly breaks a rule you must keep, and the screen before each fight says which. One of them keeps every rule you do.

    
Trinkets. Beating Veyra, Corvane, Maren, Grael and Osric each earns a trinket with a perk. Askel's is yours if you finish him, or later if you spare him, and Hadrek's if you slay him. Carry one at a time, chosen in the Trinket row on the screen before a fight. After Chapter I you may carry two, or none for a ninth card. The Trinkets page explains each one.

    
Formations. With Grael's Crown, a relic, the deck picker lets you bind two troops into one card with a shape: one behind, beside or ahead of the other. It lands both at once and costs both, and it takes two slots. Its troops cannot be carried alone until you separate it with its cross.

    
Skirmish and Alchemy. After Veyra, wandering rivals roam the rivers. Fight them in Skirmish, from the menu: each breaks a rule of its own, nothing in the story moves, and every win brings one material. Story fights pay too: breaking all three of a rival's Pentagons in your first fight with a challenger earns 5 materials, from Corvane on. Rematches pay nothing. Half the time a beaten wanderer begs: spare them for what they carry, or kill them for something else. In Alchemy, two spells you own and some materials brew a new spell for good, cheaper than both and with its own nature.

What's new

    

    

    
  





  

    
Alchemy

    
Two spells and a handful of materials make a new spell, cheaper than both and with a nature of its own. A brewed spell is yours for good. Materials come only from the wandering rivals you fight in Skirmish.

    

    

    
  





  

    
Relics

    
Things taken from the fallen. You never choose them: every relic you own is always with you.

    

    
Trinkets

    
One from each of the hardest challengers. Carry one at a time (two after Chapter I, or none for a ninth card): tap it in the Trinket row on the screen before a fight, and tap it again to put it down.
